01  Vision

Care is the operating system for the people in your life.

Not a task app. Not a health app. A person-centered layer that sits on top of your calendar, reminders and contacts, and does the remembering so you can do the caring.

🧩

Modules

Every person gets a profile built from small, switchable modules. Mood, Health, Hydration, Events, Travel, Wishlist, Medication and more. Start with three, grow to twenty. Each module is the same shape in code, so new ones ship without redesigning the app.

🏠

Home

One Today screen that gathers every module across every person and sorts it by what matters right now. You never open five profiles to find the one thing that needs you.

✦

Intelligence

Insights generate themselves the moment you land. A structured pipeline turns raw entries into a visual layer: stats, trends, actions, countdowns. Refreshed every 24 hours, or on demand.

The bet: people do not fail at caring because they do not care. They fail because caring is a memory and attention job with no system behind it.

What it replaces

The notes app, the "remind me" texts to yourself, the birthday spreadsheet, the WhatsApp thread with your sibling about Dad's tablets.

What it connects to

Apple Calendar, Reminders, Contacts, Health, Photos, Weather, Maps. Care reads what already exists and writes back where it belongs.

How it feels

Luma's warmth, BoxBox's confidence, Structured's calm. Big type, glass surfaces, per-person color auras, motion that answers every tap.

How it starts

Built for one user first, fully local. Perfected on real life for a few months. Then linked profiles, then the world.

02  The problem

Care is invisible work, and invisible work gets dropped first.

Six problems show up in different lives. They share one root cause: there is no system for the mental load of looking after people.

🧠

Mental load

Anticipating needs, remembering details, planning ahead. In most households one person carries nearly all of it, silently. It cannot be shared because it lives in one head.

🫧

Small moments decay

Relationships weaken through missed micro-moments, not big fights. "How did it go?" never asked. A worry mentioned once and never followed up. The date remembered a day late.

🥪

The sandwich

Adults caring for parents and children at the same time juggle medication, exams, appointments and school across three cities and two generations, with a chat thread as the only tool.

📡

Distance

Partners who travel. Parents in another country. Siblings in different time zones. The people who need the most deliberate care are the ones we see least.

⚡

Attention collapse

Work, feeds and notifications crowd out the quiet mental space where "things about the people I love" used to live. Prospective memory (remembering to do something later) is the first thing to go.

🗂️

Fragmentation

Birthdays in Contacts. Appointments in Calendar. Promises in Reminders. Gift ideas in Notes. Trips in Photos. Six tools, none of them organized around a person.

The insight behind Care

Every existing tool is organized around a type of information (a date, a task, a note). Care is organized around a person. Everything about Meera lives with Meera. That single shift makes the information findable, the patterns visible, and the mental load shareable.

The second insight is that most of the work should be automatic. Birthdays come from Contacts. Appointments come from Calendar. Trips come from Photos. Mood comes from a single tap on a notification. The user's job is to answer, not to enter.

What "care" means, operationally

Knowing how someone is. Remembering what matters to them. Acting at the right moment. Noticing patterns a single day hides. Every module supports one or more of these four verbs.

04  Principles

Zero-exam UX. The app asks, you answer. Never the other way round.

The biggest risk is logging fatigue. Every design decision follows one rule: reduce what the user has to type, then reduce what they have to open, then reduce what they have to remember.

The input ladder

Every piece of data enters Care through the lowest rung possible. Typing is the last resort, and it is always voice-first.

1

Passive

Imported with permission. Birthdays from Contacts. Events from Calendar. Trips from Photos. Water and sleep from Health (self only). No effort at all.

2

Ambient

Answered where you already are. A notification with three buttons. A Lock Screen widget. A Watch complication. One tap, app never opens.

3

Quick

Inside the app, one gesture. The Pulse slider sets mood and energy in one drag. Chips for symptoms. A tap on a water cup.

4

Spoken

Only for Mentions and notes. Hold to talk, on iPhone or Watch. Siri: "Remember that Meera wants to try the pottery class." Typing exists but is never required.

Fifteen seconds a day

The core loop is a morning glance at Today and one or two ambient answers. The evening wrap is three questions, skippable in one tap. Nothing else is required, ever.

Nothing to log is a good day

Empty states are calm, not guilty. "Everyone is fine. Nothing needs you today." No streaks to break, no red badges for missed days.

Push with a reason

Every nudge names the person, the reason and the one action. "Aarav's exam finished. Ask how it went." Reminders without a reason are not sent.

Private by default

Everything is local in Phase 0. When sharing arrives, visibility is set per module per person, and every adult can see what exists about them.

Read what exists, write where it belongs

Apple Calendar and Reminders stay the system of record for dates and to-dos. Care adds the person layer and writes back, two-way.

Motion answers every touch

Every tap gets a spring, a haptic and a visible state change. The app should feel like it is made of soft glass and light, not forms and lists.

05  Design language

Aura. Soft glass, living color, big confident type.

Every person has an aura: a two-color gradient that tints their profile, their orb, their cards and their notifications. The app is light and warm by default, deep and glowing at night.

References, decoded

  • Luma: giant tight headline with one gradient phrase, pastel mesh background, floating cards with real content, black pill buttons. We take the headline system, the mesh and the pill button.
  • BoxBox Club: dark surfaces lit by one strong color, huge tabular countdown numerals, glass tab bar, hero cards that feel like posters. We take the numerals, the night mode and the hero card.
  • Structured: a calm vertical timeline with time rail, colored icons per item, soft blush palette. We take the timeline for the day view and the gentle tone.
  • ShopBack: bold gradient header that fades into white cards, clear tabs. We take the "gradient header into content" transition for person profiles.
One rule to keep it premium

Spend boldness in one place per screen. One hero card, one huge number, one aura wash. Everything else is quiet glass. Two heroes on one screen is the fastest way to look like a template.

Radius and spacing (concentric rule: outer = inner + padding)

  • Screen cards 24 pt with 12 pt padding, inner tiles 12 pt. Hero card 28 pt. Chips 999. Orbs 44 pt circles. Tab bar pill 999.
  • Spacing scale 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32. Section gap is at least twice the gap inside a section.
  • Shadows for elevation, borders for structure. Cards: shadow(color: .black.opacity(0.06), radius: 24, y: 8) in light, none in night (glass carries depth).

Surfaces

  • Mesh background: MeshGradient 3x3 with the active person's aura in two corners and amber at the bottom, animated slowly with TimelineView. Opacity 0.3 in light, 0.45 in night.
  • Glass chrome: tab bar, floating action, sheet headers use Liquid Glass .glassEffect() inside a GlassEffectContainer so they morph together.
  • Cards: white at 72 percent with .ultraThinMaterial behind on light; 7 percent white on night. One hairline border at 7 percent.
  • Attention state: a 1.5 pt coral ring plus coral glow, never a red fill.

07  Modules

Twenty-two modules, one shape. New ones ship without touching the app.

A module is a Swift package that declares what it collects, how it is logged with one tap, how it renders on Today, what the AI may read, and which reminders it can send. Tier labels are baked in from day one so the paywall can be switched on later.

Quick log

One tap or one gesture. The module defines its own QuickSheet fragment and notification actions.

Today tile

A bento tile with today's state and one action. The Home agent ranks these across people.

Insight contract

A compact context pack the AI can read, and the block types it may return. Nothing else is sent.

Reminder rules

Declarative triggers with lead times, follow-ups, caps and quiet hours. The planner combines them.

08  Intelligence

Raw taps in. Typed blocks out. The screen draws itself.

This is the core feature. The model never answers in free prose. It answers in a strict JSON contract of visual blocks that SwiftUI renders directly. Insights generate on app open, cache for 24 hours, and refresh on demand.

Prompts System voice and the person-scope template

System prompt (all scopes)

You write insights for Care, an app that helps one person look after the people in their life.
You receive compact JSON about ONE scope (a person, a module, or the whole home) and must answer
ONLY with the care_insight JSON contract. No prose outside the JSON.

Voice: warm, specific, short. Like a thoughtful friend who noticed something. Never clinical.
Rules:
1. Be specific. Name days, counts, tags. Never vague encouragement.
2. Never diagnose, never name a condition, never give medical advice. If health data suggests
   a doctor might help, say exactly that in a "note" block and nothing more.
3. Never blame the user or the person. No "you should have".
4. If there is not enough data, say so in the headline and return at most one "note" block.
5. Use at most one "action". It must be something the user can do this week, and the deeplink
   must use only modules listed in allowed_modules.
6. "reminders" are optional and at most 3. Only propose a reminder when there is a clear reason.
   fire_at must be inside quiet_hours_exclusion and within the next 7 days.
7. Every claim must be traceable: add the ids you used to evidence_ids, and summarize what you
   read in "evidence" (for example "21 check-ins, 6 mentions").
8. Respect visibility: data marked private_to_user is for the user's eyes; never phrase it as if
   the person said it.
9. Plain English. No em dashes. Sentence case. Under 80 characters for headline.

Depth Caching, refresh, cost and safety
  • Generation trigger: on app foreground, for the Home scope and for the top three ranked people. Other people generate lazily when opened. Weekly runs in a BGProcessingTask.
  • Cache: Insight rows in SwiftData with generatedAt, expiresAt (24 h), modelId, blocksJSON, inputHash. If the input hash has not changed, no call is made even after expiry.
  • Manual refresh: allowed once per hour per scope; the button shows "Refreshed 6h ago" and a subtle cooldown ring.
  • Cost envelope (single user): about 6 to 10 Luna calls per day at roughly 2k input and 400 output tokens each, plus one Terra call a week. Under a few dollars a month. Multiply by users later; the schema keeps output tokens bounded.
  • Failure: network or refusal returns the previous insight with a small "could not refresh" note. Never an empty card.
  • Safety: the validator rejects any headline or text containing a blocked list of clinical terms, rejects reminders inside quiet hours, and caps actions at one. Intimacy data never enters a pack unless both partners opted in (Phase 1 concept; in Phase 0 it is simply excluded).
  • Feedback: a thumbs on each card writes feedback to the row; the next prompt includes "previous_headline" and "previous_feedback" so the model varies its angle.
  • Privacy: packs are minimal, names can be replaced with first names or aliases, no photos, no free text longer than 140 characters per item. API key in Keychain, never in source.

Depth Sync rules and permission choreography

Two-way sync rules (EventKit)

  • Care owns items it created (tagged with a URL care://… in the event or reminder). It never edits items it did not create; it links to them.
  • Every linked item has an IntegrationLink row: local entity id, external identifier, last known modification date. Conflicts resolve by latest modification; Care never deletes external items, it unlinks.
  • Imported events are proposed, not silently added: a small "3 new events for Meera from Calendar" card on Today with Add all or Review.
  • Person matching: attendee email equals contact email, or title contains the person's first name, or the calendar itself is assigned to a person ("Aarav school").

@Model final class Place    { id: UUID; person: Person?; name: String; lat, lon: Double; visitedFrom, visitedTo: Date; source: Source }
  • Why JSON payloads: each module owns its Payload type. Adding a field to Hydration never touches the shared schema. Migrations are per module and versioned with a payloadVersion tag.
  • Sync readiness: all ids are UUIDs, timestamps are set on write, deletes are soft. Turning on CloudKit later means adding the container and a sharing model, not rewriting models.
  • Ranking on device: HomeRanker scores signals by time pressure, health weight, streak length, not-yet-acted, neglect and rhythm. The model only writes words around the top results.
  • Deep links: care://person/{id}, care://person/{id}/{module}, care://person/{id}/{module}/new?…, care://today, care://timeline/{date}. Used by widgets, notifications and insight actions.

Performance and feel budget

  • Cold launch to Today under 600 ms with cached insights. Insight generation is never on the critical path.
  • All lists lazy; entries paged by month; charts downsampled to 30 points.
  • Hitches under 1 ms per frame in Instruments; springs never longer than 500 ms; haptics only on completed actions.

14  Pitch

The story we tell when it is time to sell.

Ready-made narrative for decks and stakeholder conversations. Pricing is deliberately absent; the structure for it is in place.

One line

Care is the app that makes sure you never miss the small things that matter to the people you love.

Elevator pitch

Everyone has people to look after: a partner, parents, kids, friends. The information you need to do that well is scattered across six apps and mostly in your head, and it fails exactly when life gets busy. Care gives every person in your life a profile built from small modules, imports what already exists from your calendar, contacts and photos, and asks you one quick question a day. Its intelligence reads the patterns and turns them into visual insights and perfectly timed nudges. Ten seconds a day, and people start asking "how did you remember that?"

Why now

  • Attention is the scarcest resource in a relationship, and it is shrinking.
  • Families are spread across cities and time zones more than ever.
  • Self-tracking is normal; tracking for the people around you is the natural next step and nobody owns it.
  • Language models now write with warmth for a fraction of a cent, which makes personal insight economical at scale.

Three differentiators

Person-centered, not task-centered

The only mainstream app where the unit of organization is a person. Every competitor organizes by data type.

Modules as the growth engine

New life situations become new modules, not new apps. The catalog grows monthly and keeps interest locked.

Insights that render, not read

A structured AI contract turns raw entries into stats, trends, countdowns and actions. It looks like a product, not a chat.

Objections and answers

"Is this surveillance?"Everything is private by default, sharing is per module, adults see what exists about them, and Care never uses location.
"Will people really log daily?"Most entries come from imports and one-tap notification answers. The app is designed to be answered, not opened.
"Why not just use Reminders?"Care uses Reminders. It adds the person, the reason, the pattern and the follow-up that Reminders cannot know.
"Isn't AI expensive?"A strict schema bounds output. Daily insights cost well under a cent per person per day on the small model.
Paywall structure, reserved for later

Core modules and one circle stay free forever. Plus unlocks the Home agent, weekly deep insights and the Plus modules. Pro adds professional circles and the Notes pack. Every surface already carries its tier label; the entitlement seam is one function. No purchases are wired in Phase 0.

15  Decisions and risks

Settled, open, and what could go wrong.

Settled in v2

  • Four tabs: Today, People, Timeline, You. Insights are inline, not a tab.
  • Insights auto-generate on open with a 24 hour cache and a one hour manual refresh cooldown.
  • AI returns typed blocks only; the block renderer is the core feature.
  • Phase 0 is single user, local SwiftData, no backend; linked profiles wait for CloudKit in Phase 1.
  • Apple Calendar and Reminders are systems of record; Care writes to its own calendar and per-person lists.
  • Per-person auras and the Pulse control are the signature interactions.
  • Paywall structure exists in code and UI as tiers; no purchases until Phase 2.

Open

  • Hidden modules for linked adults: fully hidden or "exists but private". Decide in Phase 1 with the partner.
  • Pulse axes: mood only, or mood plus energy on a vertical drag. Try both in M1.
  • Evening wrap default: on or off after week two, based on skip rate.
  • Travel log clustering: minimum distance from home and minimum days before a cluster counts as a trip.
  • Brand connectors: which shopping sources to unfurl beyond LinkPresentation, and when.
  • Apple Watch timing: Phase 1 or a lightweight companion in M5.

Risks

Logging fatiguePassive imports and ambient answers first; no streaks; measure the rung mix weekly.
AI says something wrong or coldStrict schema, validator, blocked terms, evidence line, thumbs feedback, previous insight kept on failure.
Feels like controlPrivate by default, transparency of existence, no location, easy leave.
EventKit edge casesCare only edits what it created; unlink instead of delete; conflicts by latest change.
Scope creepEvery module passes the one-shape, one-tap test; one new module a month after launch, never more.
